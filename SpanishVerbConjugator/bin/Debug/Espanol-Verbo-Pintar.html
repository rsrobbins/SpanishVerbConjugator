pintar - to paint

pintar: to paint
Regular first-conjugation Spanish verb
Transitive verb (takes a direct object)
Intransitive verb (does not take a direct object)

infinitive: pintar
gerund: pintando
present participle: pintando
past participle: pintado
auxiliary verb: haber

El Presente Perfecto - The Present Perfect Tense
Español Inglés Español Inglés
yo he pintado I have painted nosotros hemos pintado we have painted
tú has pintado you have painted vosotros habéis pintado you have painted
usted ha pintado you have painted ustedes han pintado you have painted
él ha pintado he has painted ellos han pintado they have painted
ella ha pintado she has painted ellas han pintado they have painted

The perfect tense in Spanish has two parts to it. The present tense of the verb haber (to have) plus the past participle. The present prefect tense expresses past action closely related to the present. The English equivalent of haber + past participle is to have done something.

This is the present perfect indicative, not the present perfect subjunctive.

El Imperfecto de Indicativo - The Imperfect Tense
Español Inglés Español Inglés
yo pintaba I was painting, I used to paint nosotros pintábamos we were painting, we used to paint
tú pintabas you were painting, you used to paint vosotros pintabais you were painting, you used to paint
usted pintaba you was painting, you used to paint ustedes pintaban you were painting, you used to paint
él pintaba he was painting, he used to paint ellos pintaban they were painting, they used to paint
ella pintaba she was painting, she used to paint ellas pintaban they were painting, they used to paint

The imperfect tense expresses actions in the past that are not seen as completed. It is used to indicate situations or actions in the past with no specific reference to their beginning or end.

The imperfect indicative tense can be translated to English as "used to paint" or "was painting."

Was: is used with singular pronouns like "I" and "he".
Were: is used with plural pronouns like "we" and "they".
However, were is also used for "you".
